Life Simulator 2D 🎮

A 2D life simulation game built from scratch with Python and Pygame. The player manages their money, hunger, energy and health while working different jobs, buying items, completing minigames and progressing through the world.

View on GitHub → ← Back to Projects

About the Project

Life Simulator 2D is one of my larger Python projects. I created it to explore how multiple game systems can work together inside one program while also developing my programming and problem-solving skills.

The player starts with a balance of £100 and must manage their hunger, energy and health while progressing through different days. Money can be earned through a range of jobs and spent on food, energy items and other parts of the game.

As the player progresses, they encounter different seasons, achievements, random events, minigames, shops and other systems that make the game more than a simple life simulator.

Problem Solving & Challenges

One of the biggest challenges was managing a large number of interconnected systems without breaking existing parts of the game. Changes to the economy, inventory, jobs or player statistics could affect other parts of the program.

I had to debug problems involving game states, variables, UI elements and interactions between different systems. This taught me to break problems down into smaller parts and test individual systems instead of trying to fix everything at once.

I also had to think about how different systems should interact. For example, items need to affect player statistics, jobs need to reward the player, and progression needs to unlock new content at the correct times.

What I Learned

Building Life Simulator 2D significantly improved my understanding of Python and game development. I learned more about game loops, event handling, UI systems, game states and organising larger programs.

The project also gave me more experience with object-oriented programming and reusable systems. Instead of treating every feature as a completely separate piece of code, I learned how systems can be designed to work together.

Most importantly, I learned how much planning and debugging is required as a project becomes larger. Fixing one problem can sometimes reveal another, so testing and breaking problems into smaller sections became an important part of development.
