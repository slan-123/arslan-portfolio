Dye Blocks

Hard

Three containers contain dyes made from blocks of type A or C. Adding one dye to another changes the blocks according to a set of rules. The goal was to reach the required final arrangement using as few swaps as possible.

💭 My reasoning

I first noticed that container 2 already contained ACA, which was its required final result. This meant there was no reason to use container 2.

I realised I could therefore focus exclusively on containers 1 and 3. After trying different combinations, I found the sequence:

3 → 1 → 3 → 1

This transforms the first and third containers into their required final states while leaving container 2 unchanged.

💡 Key observation

The biggest breakthrough was recognising that one part of the problem was already solved. Since container 2 did not need to change, I could completely ignore it and reduce the number of possible combinations I needed to consider.

✅ Answer: 3 → 1 → 3 → 1
Pattern Recognition Logic Search-Space Reduction

Aristo's Cards

Hard

Aristo claims that if one side of a card shows a vowel, the other side must always show an even number. The cards visible are E, V, 2 and 7. The task is to determine which cards must be turned over to test the claim.

💭 My reasoning

I knew that E had to be turned over because it is a vowel. If the other side showed an odd number, the claim would immediately be false.

I also had to turn over 7. If the other side was a vowel, the claim would be false.

I did not need to turn over V because the claim says nothing about what must be behind a consonant.

I also did not need to turn over 2 because the claim never says that every even number must have a vowel on its other side.

💡 Key observation

I focused on finding the cards that could actually disprove the claim instead of checking every card.

The claim is essentially vowel → even, so I only needed to check vowels and odd numbers.

✅ Answer: E and 7
Logical Thinking Conditional Logic Problem Solving

Magic Key

Medium

A magic key writes a sequence of numbers. Each press adds a new block counting down from the press number to 1. The task was to find the number at position 127.

💭 My reasoning

I noticed that each block contains a number of values equal to the block number. So after 15 blocks, the total number of values is:

1 + 2 + 3 + ... + 15 = 120

This means position 120 is the end of the 15th block. The 16th block therefore starts at position 121:

16, 15, 14, 13, 12, 11, 10, ...

Position 127 is the seventh number in this block, giving an answer of 10.

💡 Key observation

I didn't need to generate the entire sequence. I used the cumulative length of each block to jump directly to the section containing position 127.

✅ Answer: 10
Pattern Recognition Mathematical Reasoning Efficiency

Flower Planting

Medium

A robot plants flowers according to a set of instructions. It follows signs, remembers the flower it planted, and copies that flower into the next empty position.

💭 My reasoning

I started at the position marked with X and followed the robot's instructions step by step.

Each time the robot reached a sign, I recorded the flower it planted and followed where it moved next. I kept track of the flower it remembered as it moved through the row.

Following the process gave the final arrangement:

1 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 1

💡 Key observation

The important part was carefully tracking both the robot's current position and the flower it remembered. By simulating the instructions in order, I could work out the final row without having to guess the arrangement.

✅ Answer: 1, 1, 2, 3, 2, 2, 3, 2, 1, 1
Simulation Algorithms Logical Thinking

Happy Circle

Medium

Six friends need to sit around a circular table. Each friend has preferences about who they want to sit next to. The goal is to find an arrangement where everyone is happy with both of their neighbours.

💭 My reasoning

I treated the problem as a set of constraints where each person had to satisfy the preferences of the people sitting next to them.

I tried different arrangements around the circle and checked the neighbours of each person. By using the preferences to rule out unsuitable arrangements, I found an order where everyone was happy.

A → E → C → F → D → B

Because the table is circular, B is also next to A, completing the circle.

💡 Key observation

The important part was thinking about the problem as a set of constraints. Instead of only checking one person at a time, I had to make sure the entire arrangement worked together.

✅ Answer: A → E → C → F → D → B
Constraint Solving Logical Reasoning Combinatorial Thinking
