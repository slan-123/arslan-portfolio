Dye Blocks

Hard

A problem involving dyes made from three blocks of type A or C. Adding one dye to another changes each block according to a set of rules. The goal was to swap the dyes in containers 1 and 3 while leaving container 2 unchanged.

💭 My reasoning

I first noticed that container 2 already contained ACA, which was its required final result. This meant there was no reason to use container 2, because changing it would only make the problem more complicated.

I could therefore focus exclusively on containers 1 and 3. I experimented with the possible swaps and found the sequence:

3 → 1 → 3 → 1

Starting with CAC | ACA | AAC, the swaps produce ACC | ACA | AAC, then ACC | ACA | CAC, and finally AAC | ACA | CAC.

💡 Key observation

The most important step was recognising that container 2 was already correct. By identifying something that did not need to change, I could eliminate unnecessary possibilities and focus only on containers 1 and 3.

✅ Answer: 3 → 1 → 3 → 1
Pattern Recognition Logic Search-Space Reduction

Aristo's Cards

Hard

Aristo claims that if one side of a card shows a vowel, the other side must always show an even number. The task was to determine which cards must be turned over to test whether the claim is true.

💭 My reasoning

I knew that E is a vowel, so I had to turn it over. If there was an odd number behind it, the claim would immediately be false.

I also had to turn over 7. If there was a vowel on the other side, the claim would also be false.

I did not need to turn over V, because the claim says nothing about what must be behind a consonant.

I also did not need to turn over 2, because the claim never says that every even number must have a vowel on its other side.

💡 Key observation

I focused on finding the cards that could actually disprove the claim instead of checking every card.

The claim is essentially vowel → even, so I needed to check vowels and odd numbers.

✅ Answer: E and 7
Logical Thinking Conditional Logic Problem Solving

Magic Key

Medium

A magic key writes a sequence of numbers to a file. Each press adds a new block counting down from the press number to 1. The task was to find the number at position 127.

💭 My reasoning

I noticed that each press adds a number of values equal to the press count. So after 15 presses, the total number of values is:

1 + 2 + 3 + ... + 15 = 120

This means position 120 is the end of the 15th block. The 16th block starts at position 121:

16, 15, 14, 13, 12, 11, 10, ...

Therefore, position 127 contains 10.

💡 Key observation

I didn't need to generate the entire sequence. I used the pattern of the increasing block lengths to jump directly to the section containing position 127.

✅ Answer: 10
Pattern Recognition Mathematical Reasoning Efficiency

Flower Planting

Medium

A robot plants flowers according to a set of instructions. It follows signs, remembers the flower it planted, and copies that flower into the next empty position.

💭 My reasoning

I started at the position marked with X and followed the robot's instructions step by step.

Each time the robot reached a sign, I recorded the flower it planted. It then moved right to the next empty position and planted the same flower there.

Following this process gave the final arrangement:

1 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 1

💡 Key observation

I noticed that the flowers from the signs were effectively being copied into the empty spaces. By carefully following the robot's movement, I could work out the final arrangement without needing to guess.

✅ Answer: 1, 1, 2, 3, 2, 2, 3, 2, 1, 1
Simulation Algorithms Logical Thinking

Happy Circle

Medium

Six friends need to sit around a circular table. Each friend has preferences about who they want to sit next to. The goal is to find an arrangement where everyone is happy with both of their neighbours.

💭 My reasoning

I treated the problem like a puzzle where each person had to satisfy the preferences of the people sitting next to them.

I tried different arrangements around the circle and checked the neighbours of each person. By using the preferences to rule out unsuitable arrangements, I found an order where everyone was happy.

A → E → C → F → D → B

Because the table is circular, B is also next to A, completing the circle.

💡 Key observation

The important part was thinking about the problem as a set of constraints. Instead of only checking one person at a time, I had to make sure the entire arrangement worked together.

✅ Answer: A → E → C → F → D → B
Constraint Solving Logical Reasoning Combinatorial Thinking
