Problem Solving

I enjoy breaking difficult problems into smaller parts, looking for patterns and testing different approaches until I find a simple solution. Here are some computational thinking problems I've worked through.

🧪

Dye Blocks

A computational thinking problem involving dyes made from blocks of different types and rules for how the blocks change.

💭 My reasoning

I started by trying different combinations to understand how the blocks changed. I noticed that one of the positions did not actually need to change, which meant I could focus on swapping the other two positions.

💡 Key observation

Instead of trying every possible combination, I realised that the problem could be simplified by concentrating on the positions that actually affected the result.

Pattern Recognition Logic Algorithms
🔄

Swapping Problem

A problem involving repeated swaps and determining whether the final arrangement is guaranteed to have a particular result.

💭 My reasoning

I noticed that an odd number of swaps could still leave a possibility of getting the wrong arrangement. However, after an even number of swaps, the elements have effectively had the opportunity to swap back into their required positions.

💡 Key observation

The important part was not just counting the swaps, but thinking about what each swap does to the arrangement and how the parity of the number of swaps affects the result.
